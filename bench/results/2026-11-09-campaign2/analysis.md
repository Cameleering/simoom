## What was measured

**Code.**
- simoom is pinned to commit `1e50579`. The tree is built by `bench/cloud/pin-tree.sh` from `git archive 1e50579`.
- Only the benchmark's own paths (`crates/simoom-bench`, `bench/`) come from the working tree. Other agents were editing the simoom crates in parallel, and none of those edits are measured.
- Cameleer is the committed HEAD at ship time.

**Sessions.** The plan changed twice during the run, on the coordinator's instructions. The final shape:

| session | machines | what ran |
|---|---|---|
| `s1` (10:26-11:30 UTC) | srvA + load1..3 | round 1 with every scenario for `simoom`, `simoom-uring` and `simoom-builder` (builder's last scenarios moved to lane 1) |
| `lane1..3` (11:32-13:15 UTC) | 3 lanes; each lane is 1 server + 2 load nodes; srvA/B/C rotate per round | everything else, rounds 1-3 |

- **Lanes.** In round r, lane i runs on server droplet `(i - 1 + r - 1) mod 3`. Each server therefore meets a different droplet in each of its three rounds, while keeping its own two load nodes.
- **Load-node health.** No run took a load node above 60% CPU; the highest was 56.9%. Two load nodes were enough.
- **Three rounds:** the 8 simoom variants, cameleer-http-min, hyper, may-minihttp, xitca, ntex, axum, h2o, nginx and haproxy.
- **One round (labelled `(1)` in the leaderboard):** cameleer `classic`, fasthttp, netty and actix.
- **Dropped as campaign-1 laggards:** envoy, caddy, go-nethttp, kestrel, vertx and uws. Their data stays in campaign 1.
- **Round 1 scenario set:** S1, S3, S5, S7, S8, H3, CPU and COLD wherever the server supports them. S1 runs at 256 and 1024 connections; at 64 only for the simoom variants, hyper and may-minihttp.
- **Round 1 extras:** S4/S6/S10 on nginx, S6 on hyper, S6/S9 on xitca, S9 on ntex, S10 on haproxy, S6/SLEEP on cameleer-http-min.
- **Rounds 2 and 3:** S1 at 256 connections only.
- **SLO search.** The search starts from a hint (campaign 1's result, or simoom's own campaign-2 value; `bench/cloud/hints.txt`). It tries the hint, then ±30%, then bisects down to a 4% bracket, using 5 s steps.
- **Measured points.** S1 at 256 connections in round 1 measures 5 points of 20 s (50, 80, 95, 100 and 100%). Rounds 2 and 3 measure 2 points of 20 s (95 and 100%). Every other scenario measures 2 points of 15 s (90 and 100%).
- **Resolution.** Because of the 4% bracket, two servers within about 4% of each other are a tie.

**Not done:**
- The **c-8 scaling check**: this account is offered only `c-2` and `c-4` CPU-optimized sizes (`/v2/sizes`).
- S2 and S11.
- S4, S6, S9 and S10 on servers outside the extras list above.
- S1 at 64 connections outside the list above.
- Profiles beyond S1 and S3: simoom, simoom deferred, hyper and may-minihttp only.

**Incident.**

**Cost.** About 24 droplet-hours, roughly $3: 5 droplets × 3.1 h plus 4 droplets × 2.05 h.

## The ceiling everything hits

- Every server's closed-loop probe tops out at 164-178k req/s.
- The server's private interface peaks at about 408k packets/s (rx + tx) in S1, for simoom, nginx, hyper, may-minihttp, xitca and netty alike.
- S4 and HTTP/3 bulk sit at 1.90-1.95 Gbit/s.

As in campaign 1, the c-4 virtual NIC is the limit. S1 therefore ranks how close each server stays to that ceiling while keeping p99 ≤ 1 ms. Raw speed beyond the ceiling is not visible.

## The deferred path, which is how Cameleer will call simoom

`simoom-server --mode deferred` works as follows:
- The handler calls `Reply::defer` and pushes the `Responder` into a 512-thread std pool owned by the server (a Mutex and Condvar queue).
- The worker calls `Responder::send`; `deferred-stream` uses `Responder::stream` instead.
- Bodies larger than `--body-buffer 512` are read on the worker through their `BodySource`.

`--mode blocking` is `simoom::blocking`, i.e. simoom's offload pool.

| path | S1 @256 verified SLO | S3 verified SLO | CPU µs/req at 50k | ctx sw/req | syscalls/req |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| inline, fixed reply (`simoom`) | 137.7k | 148.4k | **24.9** | 0.54 | 2.65 |
| inline, built reply (`simoom-builder`) | 122.3k | 111.6k | 27.3 | 0.48 | 2.57 |
| deferred to own pool (`simoom-deferred`) | 85.7k | 81.0k | 49.6 | 1.71 | 6.83 |
| deferred + `stream` | 79.0k | 80.7k | 52.3 | 1.65 | 7.64 |
| offload pool (`simoom-blocking`) | 92.3k | 82.0k | 48.0 | 1.72 | 6.90 |
| cameleer-http-min (thread per connection) | 94.2k | - (no echo) | 34.0 | 0.91 | 1.77 |

**100 ms blocking handler, 500 clients** (closed loop; the ideal is 5,000 req/s):

| server | req/s | p50 | p99 | errors |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| simoom-deferred | **4,950** | 100.9 ms | 102.4 ms | 0 |
| simoom-deferred-stream | 4,950 | 100.9 ms | 102.7 ms | 0 |
| cameleer-http-min | 4,950 | 100.7 ms | 102.9 ms | 0 |
| simoom-blocking (offload pool, defaults) | 164,674 answered, almost all 503 | - | - | 4.15 M non-2xx |

What this shows:
- **Blocking handlers.** The deferred path handles them exactly like a thread per connection: 4,950 req/s, at a p99 103 ms that is 0.5 ms better than cameleer-http-min's.
- **Offload pool.** It is the wrong tool for long blocking handlers at its defaults: CoDel sheds almost everything as 503.
- **CPU cost.** The deferred path costs **2× inline** (49.6 against 24.9 µs/req) and **46% more than cameleer-http-min** (34.0). It is also no cheaper than the offload pool, and its syscall and context-switch counts are almost identical to it: 6.83 against 6.90 syscalls and 1.71 against 1.72 switches per request.

The profiles explain why (`raw/lane1/prof/s1/simoom-prof-deferred.*`; the srvA run at 50k req/s):
- `OffloadPool::try_submit` holds 12.9% of the samples.
- The process makes 1.33 `getrusage` calls per request. Inline makes none: those are the watchdog timing offloaded jobs.
- 3.1 futex calls per request.

**The stall watchdog has moved the deferring route to the offload pool**, so every request makes two hand-offs: the offload pool, then the server's own pool. This is bug A below. Without the watchdog the deferred path is still about 2× inline, because of the futex wake and wait per request and the extra context switch.

**Proposed changes** (simoom crate, not done here):
1. A handler that returned `Outcome::Deferred` must never count as a stall. Measure only the CPU of the call up to `defer()`, or exclude routes that defer.
2. Have `Responder::send` from a foreign thread enqueue to the loop through a lock-free MPSC queue, with one eventfd write per batch. Today it is about 0.13 eventfd writes per request, which is fine.
3. Offer a `Server::blocking_pool(n)` (or `Limits::offload_threads` plus a "no CoDel shedding" mode) sized like Cameleer's thread pool. Embedders then have a pool built for long blocking calls without a second queue.
4. For Cameleer's short routes, the cheapest correct path is a closure that is known to be non-blocking: run it inline. For routes that might block, a per-loop parked-worker hand-off with spin-then-park would cut the futex cost. Measure each against `cameleer-http-min`'s 34 µs/req.

## Where simoom is still not the best: margins, causes, proposed changes

**1. S1 at 256 connections: -14% against may-minihttp** (137.7k against 160.4k, median of 3; hyper 137.2k ties simoom).
- With 3 load nodes in session `s1`, simoom held 150.7k. Its search passed 150.7k, and both 100% points had p99 539-567 µs.
- In the 2-load lanes, simoom failed at 133.8k and 142.5k by a hair (p99 1,038 µs and 1,007 µs), and at 139k/150k with p99 spikes of 79-535 ms while p50 stayed at 154-160 µs.
- may-minihttp passed 168k in rounds 1 and 2 (p99 0.61-0.75 ms) but dropped to 136k in round 3. The server CPU was 73% busy for both, so this is not a CPU limit.
- Spikes of hundreds of ms with a flat p50 look like TCP retransmission timeouts (the minimum RTO is 200 ms) once the virtual NIC drops packets near its 408k pps limit. At 128k req/s simoom's p99 is 841 µs against may-minihttp's 545 µs at 160k: simoom has the faster median and the heavier tail.
- Proposed:
  - (a) Record `nstat TcpRetransSegs` per step in `node.sh` and confirm. This is a benchmark change; I would add it to the next run.
  - (b) In `simoom-rt`, send each response as soon as it is serialized instead of at the end of the loop turn. Also cap the connections handled per turn, so the last connection in a busy turn doesn't wait behind the rest.
  - (c) Check that adaptive busy polling doesn't delay `epoll_wait` re-arming under bursts. Compare the p99.9 of `simoom` and `simoom-uring` at 140k, both at about 2 ms.

**2. S1 at 64 connections: -3.4% against may-minihttp** (147.5k against 152.7k, one round each). This is inside the 4% bracket, so a tie. The same tail work as loss 1 applies.

**3. S5 (connection per request): -3.5% against cameleer** (23.7k against 24.5k; xitca 23.7k; one round). Also inside the bracket. simoom's probe capacity (31.8k) is the highest of the 3-round servers, so the loss is tail latency at the 5 ms SLO. The proposal is batched `accept4` with immediate read, and measuring `epoll_wait` per connection (campaign 1: 3.5 against nginx's 1.8).

**4. S9 (overload): not comparable, but a real gap.**
- S9 offers 2 × each server's own S1 SLO at 1024 connections. For simoom that is 288k req/s, far above the 166k NIC ceiling; for xitca it is 179k. The S9 rows therefore don't measure the same thing.
- What they do show: simoom has no admission control. Above the ceiling every request is late (admitted p99 210 ms, i.e. retransmits and queueing), and only 948 req/s are answered within 1 ms.
- xitca at 179k offered still answers 141k req/s within 1 ms (p99 15 ms).
- Proposed: S9 should offer one absolute rate to every server, for example 1.15 × the NIC ceiling.
- In simoom: stop reading new requests from a connection while that loop's pending responses exceed a bound. That is backpressure, not 503s. `shed_lag` was found to shed falsely (BUGS item 16), and reading-side backpressure doesn't have that problem.

**5. S10: -21% against haproxy** (legitimate p99 under attack: 456 µs against 362 µs; one round).
- simoom closed slowloris connections 68,409 times in the run, against 0 for haproxy (it holds all 10k) and 10,000 for nginx. The attacker reopens each time, so simoom churns about 900 accepts and closes per second on the same loops as the legitimate traffic. `simoom-uring` is worse: 1.9 ms.
- Proposed: partial heads no longer hold a buffer, so don't close idle half-requests until they reach the header deadline and the per-loop connection budget is under pressure. Also add a per-source reconnect rate limit, so a closed attacker cannot come straight back.

**6. Idle PSS: -45% against cameleer-http-min** (simoom-default 2.0 MB, simoom with TLS+QUIC 2.8 MB, io_uring 4.1 MB; cameleer-http-min 1.1 MB, hyper and xitca 1.4 MB).
- This is outside the owner's 5% rule. The deferred variant's 7.8 MB is the benchmark's own 512 worker stacks, not simoom.
- Proposed: break down `smaps` per mapping for `min_server` against cameleer-http-min, then:
  - make the per-loop buffer pools and timer wheel lazy, as the QUIC endpoint already is;
  - stop pre-touching the fd-table reservation;
  - have the io_uring back end size its rings to the connection count, not a fixed maximum (it is 1.3 MB above epoll).

**7. Memory per idle connection: -41% against nginx at 10k and -28% at 100k** (913 / 737 B against 538 / 528 B).
- 10k is a **regression from campaign 1's 677 B**; the 100k figure is unchanged.
- The likely cause is per-connection state added since campaign 1: lingering-close bookkeeping, the out-of-line partial-head copy slot, and the timer entry now cancelled on close.
- Proposed: measure `size_of` for the h1 connection struct at 1e50579 against 33ee716. Keep rarely used state (linger, partial head) in a side slab allocated only when used, and give idle connections no timer-wheel slot beyond one per loop bucket.

**8. Size: -29% against may-minihttp** (min_server 722 KB, bench_server 759 KB; may-minihttp 515 KB).
- may-minihttp has no HTTP/2, strict parsing or watchdog. simoom's comparable build is `--no-default-features --features http1`, budgeted at 218 KB of added code (BUGS item 22). That build was not part of this campaign; it belongs in the next COLD scenario.

**9. S4 64 KB (-3%) and S8 (-0.2%)**: ties at the NIC.

## simoom bugs found (minimal reproductions; not fixed here)

**A. The stall watchdog moves a deferring route to the offload pool.**

```sh
simoom-server --addr 127.0.0.1:18080 --no-tls --threads 2 --mode deferred &
simoom-bench load --url http://127.0.0.1:18080/ --conns 64 --rate 30000 --warmup 1 --duration 5
cat /proc/$(pgrep simoom-server)/task/*/comm | sort | uniq -c
#   5 simoom-offload threads appear; p99 86 ms, 63 µs CPU/req (WSL)
# the same with --no-watchdog: no offload threads, p99 7 ms, 57 µs CPU/req
```

The handler only calls `reply.defer()` and a condvar `notify_one`. It should never be judged a stall.

**B. TLS responses of 1 MiB hold about 2.9 MiB per connection.**

```sh
simoom-server --addr 127.0.0.1:18080 --tls-addr 127.0.0.1:18443 --cert c.pem --key k.pem --no-quic --threads 4 &
simoom-bench load --url https://127.0.0.1:18443/1m --cacert c.pem --sni bench.local --conns 64 --duration 4
# PSS: 2.3 MB idle -> 188 MB under load -> 46 MB after (plain HTTP/1.1 /1m: 3.1 MB)
```

On the droplets this is the 188-194 MB "peak PSS" of `simoom`, `simoom-uring` and `simoom-builder` in session `s1`; campaign 1 measured 20-35 MB. The TLS writer appears to encrypt a whole body into a ciphertext buffer per connection rather than streaming records under the send-buffer bound. The memory isn't returned afterwards either.

**C. (benchmark, fixed)** `simoom-bench`'s `mbit_in` counted input that a client re-offered, many times over. That explains campaign 1's 684 Tbit/s for go-nethttp's 1 MB replies. It now counts each byte once, inside the measured window only (`crates/simoom-bench/src/load.rs`). The summaries compute bulk Gbit/s from completed responses × body size.

## Benchmark changes in this campaign (`crates/simoom-bench`, `bench/`)

- **Pinning:** `pin-tree.sh` and `campaign-ctl.sh ship`/`resync`; the commit is recorded in `tree.txt`.
- **Lanes:** three lanes, with the round barrier.
- **Faster search:** `sweep --hint/--precision` and `hints.txt`.
- **Scenario control:** `PASSES`, `DEADLINE`, and a per-server scenario applicability check, so a server skips what it can't do instead of timing out.
- **Results back during the run:** incremental `pull`/`follow`.
- **Safety net:** `safety-net.sh`, a Windows scheduled task plus a detached watcher. It does a final pull, then deletes by tag and name.
- **New scenarios:** CPU (one fixed rate for every server), SLEEP (100 ms handler, 500 clients), and NICPEAK (per-second NIC packets and CPU, including steal).
- **COLD additions:** min_server plain and TLS, and cameleer-http-min plain.
- **Server modes:** `simoom-server --mode deferred|deferred-stream|blocking`.
- **New competitors:** `simoom-default` (the campaign server with default features) and `cameleer-http-min` (now with `/sleep` and `--pool`).
- **Reports:** the summary has a leaderboard; `compare-campaigns.py` is new.
- **Check before any run:** `dry-run.sh`, a control-flow check of `campaign.sh` with no machines.
